Nonhuman Theatre was the practical component of my bachelor’s thesis, developed as an attempt to explore a new approach to theatre-making in which a nonhuman entity could become the central performer.

Audience members were invited to bring water with them in any container and in any form they chose. During the performance, the water passed through different paths, containers, and conditions, encountering a variety of physical circumstances along the way. The primary performer of the piece was therefore the water itself. Depending on the conditions it encountered, the water could follow different paths and ultimately arrive at different possible endings.

Alongside this physical process, a layer of subtitles addressed themes of migration and the loss of identity experienced by migrants. The two layers of the performance—the movement of water and the narrative of migration—overlapped conceptually, drawing a parallel between water moving from one place to another and the experience of people who leave their place of origin and navigate unfamiliar spaces while carrying traces of what they have lost.
